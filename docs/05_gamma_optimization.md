# 5. Inferring gamma drive by optimization

Fusimotor (**gamma**) drive is hard to measure directly in behaving animals. The
`gammaDriveOptimization` project infers it *indirectly*: it asks **what gamma
drive would make the model reproduce a recorded Ia signal?** — and finds it by
optimization.

## The idea

```
   guess gamma parameters
          │
          ▼
   run the spindle model  ──►  predicted Ia
          │                          │
          │                     compare (cost)
          ▼                          │
   adjust parameters  ◄──────────────┘   (repeat until the fit is good)
```

An optimizer minimizes the mismatch between the model's Ia and the target. The
manuscript uses **`patternsearch` throughout**: a two-stage grid over the burst
timing — a 7×7 coarse pass, then a 5×5 refine — with a full `patternsearch` fit
of the remaining parameters at every node, followed by an 8-start polish at the
best timing. The "hybrid" in `hybridOptimizationGammaDrive_Bspline_5cp_grid_rPotential`
refers to that grid-plus-search structure, not to mixing solvers: `fmincon` is
not part of it. In the real project the target is experimental Ia data
(Taylor et al.).

## What the tutorial actually runs

The **Gamma optimization** tab calls the manuscript's *own* functions —
`runSpindleSimForOpt_Bspline_5cp` and `getIntrafusal_pCa_Bspline_5cp` (or their
`_gDynBspline` versions) from `gammaDriveOptimization` — so the pipeline is the
published one, not a re-implementation. The only change is that the **target is generated by the model
itself** from a known gamma drive, so the "right answer" is known and can be drawn
alongside the recovered result. `tutorialOptDemo.m` sets this up.

The setup matches the manuscript's final workflow
(`runOptimizedSimulation_HybridOpt_Bspline_5cp_grid.m`):

- **Extrafusal MTU:** a sinusoidal (gait-cycle) length with α drive; the fascicle
  length drives the intrafusal fibers. It does not depend on gamma drive, so it is
  computed **once** and reused on every objective evaluation.
- **Gamma static (chain):** a periodic **B-spline with 5 control points** (in pCa)
  plus a phase shift — a flexible fusimotor waveform over the gait cycle.
- **Gamma dynamic (bag):** chosen with the tab's **gamma-dynamic model** dropdown
  (`opts.gammaDynamic`):
  - **burst (on/off)** (`'pulse'`, default) — a phasic burst; here we fit its
    **magnitude**, with the ON/OFF **timing held fixed** (the manuscript
    optimizes timing in an outer grid).
  - **B-spline (5 points)** (`'bspline'`) — the manuscript's Figure 1D form: a
    periodic B-spline with 5 control points, like gamma static, with its phase
    held fixed at 0 (the manuscript grids over it). The true shape is picked
    from presets (early burst, late burst, flat) and scaled by the level box.

  The bag is strong, so the manuscript uses **low** γ-dynamic levels; the
  defaults here are low too (a true level of 10% activation, pCa 8.4).

### Fitted parameters

`x = [bag burst pCa, cp1, cp2, cp3, cp4, cp5, phase]` — the bag burst magnitude,
the five gamma-static control points, and the waveform phase (7 parameters). With
the B-spline γ-dynamic, the burst magnitude is replaced by its 5 control points,
`x = [gd1 … gd5, cp1 … cp5, phase]` (11 parameters). The tab plots the
**recovered gamma** — both the gamma-static waveform (with its control points)
and the gamma-dynamic drive — against the known truth, so you can see how
faithfully the drive is recovered.

### Objective

The cost is the manuscript's (`objFuncWithFixedTiming_Bspline_5cp_rPotential.m`;
here `compute/rPotentialCost.m`): the total receptor potential **above its
no-drive baseline** — `r - mean(r(1:10))`, floored at 0 — scored by
**mean-normalized RMSE** (each trace divided by its own mean, then RMSE), over
gait phase 0–1.5 after one start-up cycle (the dotted lines on the fit plot). The
baseline matters because a mean-normalized comparison only works if both traces
share a zero: a firing rate has one, but `r` sits at a passive level even with no
drive. One combined trace, scored once, because that is all a real recording
gives you.

### A caveat before you read anything into the recovery numbers

The burst **timing** (or the B-spline's phase) is fixed here, at the same values
used to build the target — so the fit is handed the timing for free. That makes the demo's recovery figures
unsuitable for drawing conclusions about which parameters are identifiable: the
comparison between the bag and the γ-static waveform is confounded by the bag
having been given something the chain was not.

The manuscript does not fix timing. It searches it on a 7×7 coarse plus 5×5
refine outer grid, running a full inner fit at every node and then an 8-start
polish — 82 fits — precisely because this is a hard optimization. The demo runs a
single pass so it finishes interactively. Treat it as a walk-through of the
workflow, not as evidence about identifiability.

### Notes on identifiability and speed

- **Timing** (when gamma-dynamic turns on/off) is fixed here; it sits on a sharp
  transient and is hard to identify from a single trace — a real limitation the
  manuscript handles with the outer grid / multi-start.
- **Phase** moves little in a short run; a longer simulation horizon (more gait
  cycles) gives it more to work with.
- Each objective evaluation runs the full model, so a fit takes a couple of
  minutes. Ticking **Use parallel** (on by default) spreads `patternsearch`'s 2N
  poll points across workers — 86 s down to 57 s for the burst fit and 134 s
  down to 59 s for the B-spline one, after a one-off ~35 s pool startup. Needs the Parallel Computing Toolbox; timings are
  tabulated in [07_differences_from_manuscript.md](07_differences_from_manuscript.md).
